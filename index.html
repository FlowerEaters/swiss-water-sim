      Welcome to the Swiss Water Simulator

      What would happen if you dammed a Swiss river? Find out on real terrain and real river flows.

      1. Pick a spot in a valley, or start with the Engadine example.
      2. Draw a dam across the river and choose its height, from a beaver dam to Grande Dixence.
      3. Speed up time and watch the lake fill: see which villages, roads and islands appear.

      Works best on a laptop or desktop with Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox. This is a visualisation with simplifications (see "Model assumptions"), not an engineering tool.

      Model assumptions

      This is a visualisation, not an engineering tool. It uses real terrain and real average river flows, but simplifies a lot. Here's what to keep in mind.

      Water

      • Rivers carry their long-term average flow: no floods, droughts or seasons.
      • Flows are natural (BAFU model, 1981–2000): existing hydropower diversions are ignored.
      • Rain falling inside the zone is not added (except in the rain test).
      • No groundwater, evaporation, ice, snowmelt timing or sediment.
      • Rivers flow on top of the elevation data. Laser surveys see the water surface, not the riverbed, so real channels are a little deeper.
      • Existing lakes are flat surfaces in the data, with no depth. A river flowing into a large lake first spreads a thin layer over it before flowing out (real lakes buffer flows in a similar way).
      • The simulation uses 8 m cells, so streams narrower than about 8 m are smeared out.

      Dams

      • Dams appear instantly, are completely watertight and never fail.
      • Water only leaves over the crest: no spillway gates, bottom outlets or turbines.
      • Overflow follows the standard weir formula.

      Time and the zone

      • Above 1 hour per second, lakes fill by their volume (inflow minus overflow) with a flat surface; rivers outside the lakes are paused.
      • Only the chosen zone (6–16 km wide) is simulated. Its edges are walls, except where valleys naturally leave the zone. A lake can't grow beyond the zone; the app warns when it reaches an edge. Areas outside Switzerland have no elevation data and act as high walls.
      • Incoming rivers and their flows are found automatically from the BAFU river network; very small trickles (under 0.03 m³/s) are left out. Some rivers have no name in our data and show as "unnamed".
      • "Under water" means at least 30 cm deep in a dam's lake. Buildings, roads and places come from swisstopo's base map; bridges and tunnels are not counted.
